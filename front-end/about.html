PIN

PIN is a national Impact Investment/Philanthropic community. We are built around a core mission: using Impact Investing to mentor the Next Generation and prepare them to steward wealth and create real Impact. Monthly meetings follow an Educate, Invest, Network format — an expert speaker, a showcase of vetted investments, and time to network with other like minded individuals. Members form Roundtables around philanthropic issues like Education, Poverty, Health, and the Arts and collaborate around vetted and researched solutions in their areas of interest. Members track investments through an online dashboard, and the network brings together investors, social entrepreneurs, and professional advisors.

Meetings are streamed and recorded online, with private virtual networking rooms, extending participation to members across the country and internationally. A PIN Entrepreneur Network and affiliated accelerators feed a pipeline of vetted deal flow into each roundtable, and a donor-advised fund with a field-of-interest fund for every roundtable directs capital toward the causes members care about most.

History of PIN

Curt Bassett founded PIN after two decades leading family office services for Merrill Lynch’s wealthiest clients out of New York.

In 2016, Zions Bank — a PIN institutional member — hired Curt as SVP to build a similar service in-house, with plans to keep running PIN alongside it. Banking regulation had other plans: after a final showcase event at Zions’ headquarters in November 2017, compliance ruled PIN couldn’t continue inside the bank, and PIN was put on hold.

In 2018, Curt left Zions and wrote The Third Impact: The Revolutionary Use of Impact Investing to Bridge Wealth Management and Worth Management, on using Impact Investing to build a family’s human capital and prepare the next generation for inheritance. He launched it at a UN Impact Investing conference in December 2019, followed by a second edition and talks at wealth conferences nationwide.

That work led to consulting engagements with billionaire families across the country — several of whom encouraged Curt to bring PIN back.
